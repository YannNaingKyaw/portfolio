02 Selected work

Four engagements, and what they had to survive.

Customer names withheld where the work sits under NDA. Each case is told the same way: the problem, my part, the approach, the scale, and what keeps it running.

ISP core · National scale

MPLS core and international gateway build-out #

Problem
Subscriber traffic shifted to video and CDN-hosted content, and the backbone had to grow without breaking existing services.
My role
Senior Core Network Engineer — owned the core and IGW design decisions, and was the L3 escalation point when the national network broke.
Trade-off
Every change landed on live subscriber traffic, so capacity and new peers had to be added without a cutover that could take the core down.
Approach
MPLS core on IS-IS and MP-BGP with traffic engineering and QoS; IGW built with eBGP/iBGP peering to global CDNs; POP expansion and multi-service platforms for redundancy.
Scale
160G to 400G backbone upgrades; networks carrying millions of subscribers.
Kept running
Capacity planned ahead of saturation, redundant paths per POP, and named escalation ownership when the country notices an outage.

Private cloud · Enterprise

Data centre replacement on Cisco HyperFlex and UCS #

Problem
An end-of-life server estate had to be consolidated, with a way to add capacity in small steps instead of whole racks.
My role
Network and solution engineer on the build, later technical consultant lead on the design — platform and sizing choices were mine to defend to the customer.
Trade-off
Growth had to come in small increments on a fixed data-centre footprint, which decided HCI over a conventional SAN-and-blades refresh.
Approach
Cisco HyperFlex HCI on UCS with VMware vCenter, software-defined storage for growth by node, ACI for policy-driven fabric, plus workload migration and hardening plans.
Scale
Sized per site, with capacity added node by node instead of by whole rack.
Kept running
Multi-tenant segmentation across VLANs, VPNs and firewall policy, with the hardening plan applied before migration, not after.

Data centre fabric · Cisco ACI

ACI fabric for a multi-tenant private cloud #

Problem
Tenant separation lived in VLANs and firewall rules spread across several devices, so onboarding one new tenant meant a change window on each of them.
My role
Designed and delivered the fabric — tenant model, policy, and the path off the old one-VLAN-per-tenant design.
Trade-off
Existing workloads had to keep talking throughout, so tenancy and contracts were agreed first and workloads moved tenant by tenant instead of in one cutover.
Approach
Leaf-spine ACI with tenants, VRFs, bridge domains and endpoint groups carrying the separation, contracts stating what may talk to what, and UCS plus hypervisor integration so new workloads inherited policy instead of being hand-built.
Scale
A leaf-spine fabric serving the data centre, with tenants onboarded incrementally.
Kept running
Separation is readable policy rather than a chain of device configs only one engineer understands, so adding a tenant stops being an outage-risk event.

Smart City · Surveillance & AI

Video platform carrying AI inference at the edge #

Problem
Urban surveillance with AI analytics needs compute near the cameras, without putting cameras and sensors on the office network.
My role
Network and platform design across edge sites and the data centre, integrating with the VMS and AI analytics stack.
Trade-off
Inference had to sit near the cameras without putting cameras and IoT on the office network or backhauling every stream to the data centre.
Approach
A segmented video and IoT plane, GPU-enabled inference at the edge and in the data centre, AI VMS (Eocortex, Scylla AI), all under central management.
Scale
Multi-site cameras, IoT sensors and inference nodes.
Kept running
Video and IoT isolated from office traffic, with high-availability links from edge to data centre to private cloud.

05 Operations

The work after the deployment.

Most infrastructure fails in month nine, not on cutover day. These are the habits that keep it alive.

01

Change windows with a written rollback

Every change carries its own undo step and a tested way back. If the rollback is not written down, the change is not ready.

02

Evidence, not opinions

Preventive maintenance ends with captured output — device health, service state, capacity headroom — filed so the next round compares against something real.

03

Read-only by default

During audits and PM rounds I observe and record. Nothing gets changed to make a dashboard look better than the network is.

04

Specifications that survive procurement

Technical specs, TOR responses and BOMs checked line by line against the source quote, so what is bought is what was designed.

05

Monitoring before handover

Zabbix, SNMP and flow data in place before the customer takes the keys, so the first alarm is not the first warning.

06

One conversation with the customer

Plain language in the report, technical depth in the appendix. Whoever signs the cheque should not need a translator.
